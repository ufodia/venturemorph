# Growth and experiments

## Diagnose one current constraint

| Situation | Question | Possible next observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea only | Does an identifiable person experience the problem enough to act? | Problem interviews or manual service trial |
| Visits without first value | Can suitable users reach the core benefit? | Observe first-use sessions |
| First value without repeat use | Is the benefit recurring, reliable, and timely? | Return by cohort and natural use cycle |
| Repeat use without revenue | Is the payer, value unit, or willingness to pay wrong? | Transparent pricing or paid pilot |
| Reliable value with low discovery | Can a reachable channel attract suitable users affordably? | Bounded channel pilot with quality measurement |
| Marketplace imbalance | Can both sides find viable matches? | Supply, demand, and fulfilled matches by segment |

State competing explanations when data cannot distinguish them. Avoid daily retention for annual tasks, app downloads for web-only products, or viral loops for private utilities without justification. Incomplete cohorts cannot establish poor retention.

## Improve the idea within constraints

Connect each of up to three improvements to a specific problem, changed behavior, and outcome. Consider reliability, onboarding, audience focus, scope reduction, manual delivery, and friction removal alongside new features.

Favor the current constraint or consequential uncertainty. Explain dependencies and effort in explicit units where feasible. Label estimates. A generic impact-effort score is not evidence.

## Describe a real growth mechanism

Specify who discovers the product, the reachable channel, first value, reason to return, and how use can lead to future discovery or value. Explain payer and cost assumptions. A loop needs an actual feedback connection.

For a channel specify audience, message, initial action, core value event, repeat behavior, resource cap, and brand consequence. "Do SEO" or "post on TikTok" is insufficient without a reachable audience and testable mechanism.

## Define the experiment contract

- **Decision:** Which choice changes with the result?
- **Hypothesis:** Which intervention changes which behavior for which audience, and why?
- **Comparison:** Current experience or alternative; explain learning limits if absent.
- **Metric:** Event, numerator, denominator, unit, cohort, and observation window.
- **Baseline:** Compatible evidence or an explicit baseline collection step.
- **Threshold:** Proposed decision rule linked to feasibility/value; label assumptions and agreement status.
- **Sample/timebox:** Recruitment, exposure, timing, and limits. Distinguish a planning cap from a statistically justified sample size.
- **Cost cap:** User's money, time, and capacity. Unknown budget means a provisional low-cost plan, not spending authorization.
- **Guardrail:** Main possible harm and observable stopping condition.
- **Actions:** Continue, change, stop, and inconclusive; include any implications for brand.

Make outcome rules mutually exclusive and state their precedence. A triggered stopping guardrail overrides a positive primary metric. Missing exposure or outcome data is inconclusive unless an independently observed stopping condition already applies.

Do not infer causal lift from uncontrolled before/after data or small qualitative samples. Interview enthusiasm is not payment evidence. Clicks on a transparent test page measure expressed interest under its conditions, not purchase or retention.

Without operational authorization, produce the plan. Do not launch campaigns, contact users, publish pages, or collect data.

## Interpret results

Check definitions, missing data, selection bias, cohort maturity, novelty, seasonality, concurrent changes, and predetermined rules. Use appropriate uncertainty. Preserve inconclusive results and propose the smallest useful follow-up. Never rewrite a threshold after seeing results to manufacture success.
